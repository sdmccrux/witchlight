Once every eight years, a traveling extravaganza known as the Witchlight Carnival visits your world. You carry a dim memory of sneaking past its gates as a child without buying a ticket. A night of Impossible wonders that ended in quiet heartbreak when something precious of yours was lost. You tried to find it, but the elven carnival owners where decidedly unhelpful. As the years passed, the ache faded and you gave less and less thought to those childhood events. Yet now, for reasons you can't explain, the longing to retrieve that which you have lost has resurfaced, as though an old spell has faded away, allowing you to feel the loss as sharply as if it happened yesterday.

The Witchlight Carnival has returned. You find yourself standing near a ticket booth by the entrance at twilight, as the carnival is about to open. Two strangers stand beside you wearing your same troubled look. Without knowing how or why, you sense that they each have lost something too. Perhaps fate has brought you together.

CUSTOMS

Rule of Hospitality

When a friend, an enemy, or a stranger enters your home, you are expected to be gracious and accommodating to them until such time as they prove, by their words or actions, undeserving of such hospitality.

Rule of Ownership

You must not steal from a friend, an enemy, or a stranger. To take something which does not belong to you without the rightful owner's permission is a crime and an unforgivable breach of etiquette.

Rule of Reciprocity

When a friend, an enemy, or a stranger offers you a gift, you are obliged to accept it and offer something of comparable value (be it a gift or a service) in return. Such reciprocation need not happen immediately.

WHAT WAS STOLEN FROM YOU?

Roll 1d8 or choose for yourself during Session Zero

D8 YOUR STOLEN PIECE THE LINGERING MARK ON YOUR CHARACTER
1 Ability to Keep Secrets Whenever someone presses you or asks directly, you find yourself unable to hold back a secret.
2 Ability to Smile You feel laughter and warmth inside, yet your face is physically unable to form a smile.
3 Artistic Creativity You cannot draw, paint, or compose original music. The creative spark vanishes when you try.
4 Cherished Doll or Toy A beloved childhood doll or stuffed animal was misplaced that night at the fair.
5 Handwriting Everything you write by hand is an incomprehensible scrawl that requires magic to decipher.
6 Sense of Direction You cannot tell which way is north and easily lose your bearings when navigating.
7 Sense of Fashion You have no eye for style whatsoever, chronically mismatching colours, patterns, and attire.
8 Three Inches of Height You've always known deep down that you are three inches shorter than you ought to be.
